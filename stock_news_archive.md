### 🚨 THE BIG STORY

Kevin Warsh's Fed just served the market its clearest rate-hike warning yet. At the June 17-18 FOMC meeting — Warsh's first as chair — rates were held steady (as expected), but the dot plot revealed 9 of 18 members see at least one hike before year-end 2026. The market had priced in a benign "hold and watch" regime under the new chair. It got a slow-motion hiking signal instead — the first credible one since 2023.

The initial reaction was brutal. On June 16 (Monday), when Warsh's hawkish tone first leaked through bond markets, the Dow dropped 500+ points. On June 17 (Wednesday, the decision day), the S&P 500 fell 1.21% to 7,420 and the Nasdaq shed 1.34% to 26,021. Bond yields surged. Then came the curveball: on June 18 (Thursday — last trading day of the week), the US and Iran signed a framework peace agreement brokered by Pakistani PM Shehbaz Sharif. The MOU enters into force "with immediate effect" — Iran reopens the Strait of Hormuz, the US lifts its naval blockade, and Iran's nuclear program gets dismantled. Oil cratered, risk assets ripped, and the PHLX Semiconductor Index exploded +7.9% — its biggest single-session surge in over a year.

My take: The Warsh Fed is a regime change, not a one-meeting blip — 9/18 dots pointing higher is a structural shift. The Iran deal bought a rally, but PCE data on Thursday (Wells Fargo est. 4.1% YoY headline) and Micron's earnings Wednesday are the real tests. If PCE runs hot AND MU disappoints, the 7,500 level on the S&P cracks and the September lows come into view.
