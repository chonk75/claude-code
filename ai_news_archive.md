📌 Headline: Hangzhou court rules firing employees solely for AI replacement is illegal in China
📝 Summary: The Hangzhou Intermediate People's Court published a "typical case" April 28 (covered widely April 30 – May 1) confirming that a tech company unlawfully dismissed a QA supervisor surnamed Zhou after replacing his role with a large language model. Zhou, hired in 2022 at 25,000 yuan/month, was offered a downgrade to a 15,000 yuan role (a 40% pay cut), which he refused; the company then terminated him citing AI-driven role reduction. The court ruled the company's automation choice was a "voluntary move to stay competitive" and could not be used to "shift the risks" of advancing technology onto employees, finding that AI deployment does not meet the legal threshold of a "major change" justifying termination. The decision is being widely cited as the first concrete Chinese precedent that employers must negotiate, retrain, or reassign before using AI deployments to justify layoffs.
